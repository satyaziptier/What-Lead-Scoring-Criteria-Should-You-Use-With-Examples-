What Lead Scoring Criteria Should You Use? (With Examples)

Good lead scoring criteria look at two things: fit (is this the right kind of customer?) and intent (are they ready to buy?). Give each answer 0 to 100 points, combine the two, and sort leads into High, Medium or Low. The examples below show what should earn points and what shouldn't.

VP Vijay Satya Prakash November 13, 2026

What is lead scoring?

Lead scoring gives each lead a number or a grade, so your sales team knows who to call first. Oracle describes it as "an objective ranking of one sales lead against another". In other words, it puts your leads in order.

Most scoring systems look at two things. Fit asks: is this the right kind of person and company for what you sell? Intent asks: are they ready to buy now, based on what they do and say?

Some tools show these two parts separately, as a letter and a number. In HubSpot's combined score, the letter (A to C) shows fit and the number (1 to 3) shows engagement. Salesforce calls the fit part a grade and the interest part a score.

Scoring is not the same as qualification. Qualification decides if a lead is worth chasing at all, and our lead qualification guide covers it. Scoring decides which of those leads your team calls first.

Why does lead scoring matter for inbound teams?

Lead scoring matters because buyers make most of their decision before they talk to anyone in sales. If your team calls leads in the order they arrive, your best buyers end up waiting in line.

Buyer research backs this up. 6sense surveyed more than 4,000 buyers for its 2025 Buyer Experience Report. It found that 94% of buying groups had already ranked their favorite vendors before contacting any of them. That early favorite won 80% of the time, according to 6sense's release.

Sales reps also have less time than you might expect. Salesforce's State of Sales 2026 report surveyed 4,050 sales professionals. The average seller spends only 40% of their time on selling, per Salesforce's announcement.

So buyers decide early, and reps have limited hours. A lead score tells your team where to spend those hours first.

What criteria should a lead score include?

A lead score should include three kinds of criteria: fit, intent and negative points. Fit comes from facts the lead shares, such as a work email, job title, company size and country. Intent comes from what the lead does, asks and says.

Many teams take their intent criteria from BANT (budget, authority, need, timeline). It's a simple sales checklist for testing whether a deal is real. Our BANT guide explains each part with example questions.

Negative points matter too, because they take points away when a lead is a poor fit. In Adobe's Marketo Engage scoring guide, a company with fewer than 10 employees loses 10 points. So does a lead with no activity in 90 days.

How is a lead score calculated?

A lead score is calculated in one of two ways: add up points, or average and weight them. Either way, you finish by comparing the score to a cutoff, which decides what happens next.

Adding up points is the simpler method. Each rule adds or takes away points, and the total is the score. In Adobe's Marketo example, a demo request earns 50 points, a white paper download 5 and a trade show visit 15. The lead goes to sales at 50 points.

The problem is that totals can keep growing. HubSpot solves this with limits, and its help guide shows a total limit of 100, split into 60 and 40 for two groups.

Averaging and weighting keeps every lead on the same 0 to 100 scale, so a score of 70 means the same thing on every campaign. It works in four steps:

  1. Give every possible answer 0 to 100 points. For example, a work email earns 100 and a personal email earns 30.
  2. Average the points in each group. This gives you a fit score and an intent score, each out of 100.
  3. Decide how much each group counts, so the two add up to 100%. For example, fit counts 40% and intent 60%.
  4. Compare the final score to your cutoffs. In the examples below, 70 or more is High, 40 to 69 is Medium, and under 40 is Low.

What happens to old activity and missing answers?

Old activity should count for less, and skipped questions usually shouldn't count at all. Both choices can change a lead's grade, so decide them on purpose.

Score decay means old actions slowly lose their points. In HubSpot, you can reduce points every 1, 3, 6 or 12 months. At 50% a month, a visit worth 10 points is worth 5 a month later. Salesforce does this automatically in its Einstein behavior score.

Skipped questions need a rule too. In the example above, Sam didn't share his company size, so that question was left out of his average. If it had counted as zero, his fit score would drop from 35.0 to 23.3. That would punish him just for skipping an optional question.

Which signals look like intent but aren't?

Pricing questions, feature comparisons and polite interest look like buying signals, but often they aren't. Students, researchers and competitors ask the same questions every day.

If these add points, your High list fills up with people who were never going to buy. Your sales team soon learns to ignore the alerts. Then a real buyer waits as long as everyone else.

Watch out for these look-alikes when you set your intent rules:

  • A pricing question on its own
  • A feature comparison on its own
  • Vague interest, such as "maybe later"
  • A job title or department on its own
  • General questions about how the product works

Look for clear evidence, not just the topic of a question. A pricing question counts when it comes with a deadline, a budget or a real problem. A job title counts toward fit, but it doesn't show buying power until the person says they approve the purchase. Use one simple test: would a rep do something different because of this?

Should you use rule-based or predictive lead scoring?

Start with rule-based scoring, where your team sets the points, and move to predictive scoring once you have enough data. In predictive scoring, software learns the points from your past deals. Rules are easy to understand from day one. Predictive models can spot patterns people miss, but they need history to learn from.

Predictive models can be very accurate with the right data. A 2025 study in Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence tested 15 models on 16,600 sales records from one B2B software company. Don't expect the same result straight away. The study used one company's data, and its best clues were where the lead came from and its status in the CRM. Your system may record those differently.

Whichever method you choose, use the time it saves well. In a 2026 Gartner survey of 210 sales leaders, AI saved sellers 4.8 hours a week on average. Teams that put those hours into higher-value work were 3.1 times more likely to beat their goals for turning leads into sales opportunities, per Gartner's release.

What are lead scoring best practices?

The first of the lead scoring best practices is to change one setting at a time. Points, weights and cutoffs all affect the same final score. Change three at once, and you won't know which one helped.

Give each change a week of real leads before you judge it. Then keep these habits:

  • Only give points for things you'd act on. If two job titles get the same follow-up, give them the same points.
  • Test new fit rules before they count. Set fit to 0%, check the grades it would give, then turn it up.
  • Don't count skipped questions as zero. That punishes the visitors who chatted most naturally.
  • Ask for real interest before High. A perfect profile with no buying signal is the right person at the wrong time.
  • Start with a high bar. Adobe suggests passing leads to sales at 100 points when you're new, so one email open can't qualify a lead.
  • Match each grade to a reply time. In Oracle's example, A1 and B1 leads get a reply within 24 hours, and A2 to B3 leads within 48.
  • Check how your grades are spread. If almost every lead is High, your cutoffs are too low to be useful.

A good score loses its value if nobody acts on it quickly. In a 2011 HBR study of 2,241 companies, only 37% replied to web leads within an hour. The average reply took 42 hours, per Harvard Business Review. Our guides to speed to lead and lead routing cover what to do once a lead scores High.

What should you test in any lead scoring tool?

Ask the same six questions of any lead scoring tools you consider, including ours:

  • What gets scored? Company scores suit teams that sell to a list of named accounts. Person scores suit fast follow-up on inbound leads.
  • What feeds the score? Forms, page visits and conversations each show a different part of a buyer's interest.
  • Can you see the math? Ask the vendor to score one of your real leads in front of you.
  • Can a weak signal hold back a strong one? You want a way to stop a perfect profile with no interest from being flagged as urgent.
  • Where does the score go? Check if it reaches your CRM, an email alert, a CSV file or all three.
  • How is it priced? Paying per seat, per conversation, per result or by custom quote adds up very differently.

The first question matters because many people buy together. Forrester's 2026 State of Business Buying research says a typical purchase involves 13 people inside the company and nine outside influencers, per Forrester's release. A person score catches the one who contacts you, while a company score tracks the whole group.

FAQs

How do I score inbound leads?

Give each answer a lead shares 0 to 100 points, and average them into a fit score and an intent score. Decide how much each counts, so the two add up to 100%. Then compare the result to cutoffs, such as 70 for High and 40 for Medium.

What's the difference between lead scoring and lead grading?

A grade usually shows fit, like job title and company size, as a letter. A score usually shows interest as a number. HubSpot's combined score uses both: A to C for fit and 1 to 3 for engagement.

What's the difference between lead scoring and lead qualification?

Lead qualification decides if a lead is worth chasing at all. Lead scoring puts those leads in order, so sales knows who to call first. Many teams qualify with a checklist like BANT, then use a score to set the order.

What score should trigger a handoff to sales?

There's no single right number, because it depends on how you give points. Adobe's Marketo example passes leads to sales at 50 points and suggests 100 if you're just starting. On ZipTier's 0 to 100 scale, the default High cutoff is 70.

Should a pricing question raise a lead's score?

Not on its own, because students, researchers and competitors ask about price too. Count it when it comes with a deadline, a budget or a real business problem.

How accurate is AI lead scoring?

It can be very accurate when it learns from a company's own history. A 2025 study in Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence found one model was right 98.4% of the time on 16,600 records from one B2B software company. Your results will depend on how much data you have and how clean it is.

Which AI chatbot platforms can capture, score and route high-intent leads?

Several platforms qualify or score website leads, but they score different things. Some grade each person from what they say in the chat, while others score whole companies from website visits. Compare them on what feeds the score, whether you can see the math and where the result goes.

In a nutshell

Score fit and intent separately, then combine them in a way your sales team understands. Only give points for things that would change what your team does next, and take points away for poor fit. Let clear buying signals, not pricing questions or page views, move a lead to High. Start with simple rules, try predictive scoring once you have enough data, and change one setting at a time.
